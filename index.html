JMO 2008 B3 · fully illustrated angle proof

Find an expression for ∠DRQ in terms of x

We will identify the corresponding sides of the congruent rectangles, transfer the angle x using parallel lines, form an isosceles triangle, and then subtract from a right angle.

Final answer: x2°

Original problem and the objects we need

There are two rectangles

The lower rectangle is ABCD. The tilted rectangle is APQR. They share the vertex A.

Point D lies on the side PQ, so P, D and Q are on one straight line.

We are given ∠PDA=x° and must find ∠DRQ.

In an angle name, the middle letter is the vertex. Thus ∠PDA is at D, while ∠DRQ is at R.

1

Decode what “congruent rectangles” tells us

Congruent means exactly the same shape and exactly the same size. The order of the letters tells us which vertices correspond:

A↔A, B↔P, C↔Q, D↔R
AB corresponds to AP
so AB = AP.
BC corresponds to PQ
so BC = PQ.
CD corresponds to QR
so CD = QR.
DA corresponds to RA
so DA = RA.

The equality needed later is DA=RA

This does not come from the picture merely looking symmetrical. It follows from the stated congruence and the correspondence of the vertices.

2

Why the angle at A is also x

A rectangle has opposite sides parallel. In rectangle APQR,

AR∥PQ

Because D lies on PQ, the ray DP runs along the same straight line as PQ. The segment AD crosses the two parallel lines AR and PQ, so AD is a transversal.

The angles ∠PDA and ∠DAR lie between the parallel lines and on opposite sides of the transversal. They are alternate interior angles, so they are equal:

∠PDA = ∠DAR ∠DAR =x°
Why do alternate angles have the same size?
Parallel lines point in exactly the same direction. The angle made when AD meets PQ is therefore the same acute turn as the angle made when AD meets AR. The familiar “Z-shape” in the highlighted diagram is a way of recognising this fact.
3

Recognise triangle ADR as isosceles

We now know two facts about triangle ADR:

DA=RA and ∠DAR=x°

A triangle with two equal sides is an isosceles triangle. The equal sides here meet at A, so A is the apex and DR is the base.

Equal sides lie opposite equal angles:

∠ADR = ∠ARD

DA is opposite the angle at R, while RA is opposite the angle at D. Since DA = RA, those two opposite angles are equal.

4

Calculate each base angle of triangle ADR

Let each equal base angle be y°. The three interior angles of a triangle total 180°:

x+y+y=180 x+2y=180 2y=180−x y = 180−x2 =90−x2

Therefore, in particular,

∠ARD = (90−x2)°
5

Use the right angle at R

APQR is a rectangle, so every corner of it is a right angle. Therefore

∠ARQ=90°

The ray RD lies inside this right angle and divides it into the two adjacent angles ∠ARD and ∠DRQ:

∠ARD + ∠DRQ =90°

Substitute the base angle already found:

(90−x2) + ∠DRQ =90 ∠DRQ = 90 − (90−x2) ∠DRQ =x2°

Common traps in this problem

1. Matching the wrong sides

The needed equality is AD = AR, not AD = AP. The correspondence comes from the ordering ABCD ↔ APQR.

2. Treating D as a corner of APQR

D is merely a point on the side PQ. The corners of the tilted rectangle are A, P, Q and R.

3. Subtracting x directly from 90°

The large part of the right angle at R is not x. It is the base angle 90° − x/2.

4. Forgetting the middle-letter rule

∠DRQ is at R because R is the middle letter. It is the small angle between RD and RQ.

Final answer

The required angle is

∠DRQ = x2°

The essential chain is: parallel sides give ∠DAR = x°; congruence gives DA = RA; the isosceles triangle gives ∠ARD = 90° − x/2; and the remaining part of the right angle at R is x/2.
